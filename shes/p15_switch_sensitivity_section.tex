\section{A pinned named-switch outage sensitivity on the IEEE 123-bus feeder}
\label{sec:p15-switch}
We investigated a documented, named line-switch element in the pinned OpenDSS IEEE 123-bus model (upstream revision \texttt{5005c668a72d20775f4c2d060feebb2866ba1d38}; \texttt{dss-python} version \texttt{0.15.7}). The extraction identified eight enabled elements with the explicit \texttt{Line.sw} naming convention. We selected the first, \texttt{Line.sw1}, and compared the published model with a fresh compilation in which that element was disabled. The experiment is an \emph{outage sensitivity}, not a proposed or operationally permitted restoration sequence.

Both snapshot power-flow solves converged with controls disabled. The published model yielded per-unit voltage magnitudes at bus 60 of \(0.942465,0.979794,0.960244\) and at bus 83 of \(0.945853,0.983088,0.961112\) on phases 1--3, respectively. After disabling \texttt{Line.sw1}, all six reported phase-voltage magnitudes were zero, despite solver convergence. Hence convergence alone is not evidence of viable supply or restoration feasibility. A zero reported voltage is consistent with the isolated state but is not a substitute for an independently verified phase-aware source path.

The model audit records transformer terminal buses, conductor counts, and phase counts without inventing a one-to-one winding transfer. Neither bus-level adjacency nor terminal-current magnitude establishes regulator phase transfer, energized continuity, installed CT polarity, or protection-zone ownership. Signed OpenDSS element-terminal currents, where extracted, follow the simulator's \emph{into-element} sign convention; they are not installed CT measurements. Thermal adequacy cannot be claimed without verified equipment ratings and relevant operating limits. No physical restoration or protection safety certificate was issued.

\paragraph{Adversarial validation.}
Nine regression tests, including earlier phase-topology tests, reject phase-permutation-as-identity, an open-conductor path, and unlisted or disabled elements masquerading as eligible named switches. Source-pinned GitHub Actions independently checked the two scenario records, finite model voltage values, source revision, and fail-closed safety flags. The reproducibility evidence is stored under \texttt{shes/switch\_sensitivity\_p15.py} and the P15 workflow. This test does not establish post-switch fault-current redistribution or full thermal and protection coordination, which require a subsequent phase-transfer and fault-probe analysis.
